What future maps can show

Spatial data is useful when it helps explain why patterns are different across places. These are the kinds of map layers and interactions this page is designed to support.

📍

Stations and sites

Monitoring points, sample locations, survey sites, or other fixed places that serve as anchors for spatial analysis.

🧭

Regional layers

Boundaries, zones, regions, watersheds, districts, or other spatial layers that give observations geographic context.

🟣

Detections and intensity

Points, clusters, counts, rates, or gradients that show where events are concentrated and how patterns vary by place.

Possible map tools

  • 1 Layer toggles for turning stations, regions, detections, and reference boundaries on or off.
  • 2 Tooltips and labels that explain what each point or region represents without crowding the map.
  • 3 Filters by time, category, region, dataset, or project so users can narrow the story.
  • 4 Small summaries beside the map that connect spatial patterns to chart-based evidence.

Recommended direction

Keep this page as a polished project landing page until the first map is ready. Once a real map project exists, this page can either become the map itself or become a map gallery that links to individual map stories.

My recommendation is to use this page as a map project hub, similar to the visualization gallery, while each major map gets its own dedicated page when it becomes interactive and data-heavy.
